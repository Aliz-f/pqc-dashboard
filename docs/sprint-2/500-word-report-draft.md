## Sprint goal and increment
Sprint 2 responds to the largest customer's proposed change to the post-quantum cryptography transition agreement. Instead of focusing mainly on internal adoption, the dashboard now explains customer-specific PQC readiness and potential Harvest-Now-Decrypt-Later (HNDL) exposure by data class. The customer proposes 31 December 2027 as its deadline, replacing the previous end-of-2030 target; this amendment remains pending. The V2 React prototype extends Sprint 1's executive, connection-security, and migration views with a customer-report preview, migration-blocker analysis, fallback-exception register, and customer-scoped traffic-exposure information.

## Planned work and verified delivery
The initial Sprint 1 dataset contained twelve synthetic connections: eight ML-KEM and four classical, giving 66.7% adoption. V2 preserves that baseline and identifies five connections carrying the customer's data: three ML-KEM and two classical, yielding 60% customer adoption. The synthetic report totals 300 GB across personal and operational data, with 60 GB associated with classical key establishment (20%). The breakdown is 40/100 GB personal data (40%) and 20/200 GB operational data (10%). These are illustrative transferred-volume associations, not proof of intercepted traffic. Fifteen implementation and desk-verification tasks have source-backed evidence and are marked Done in Jira; parent stories and the sprint-wide review continue through their own acceptance process.

## Stakeholders, decisions, and change
Emma needs a concise customer-safe report for contract discussions. Sarah needs potential exposure explained without interpreting classical fallback as a detected attack. Michael must balance migration urgency against operational continuity. One customer connection in the synthetic example awaits a replacement expected in 2028, creating a potential conflict with the proposed 2027 deadline. The V2 interface shows this dependency while describing workarounds, alternative routing, and escalation as discussion topics rather than authorized actions. The product retains internal security and migration views, adds separate customer reporting, and avoids claiming that the pending amendment has been accepted. Formal prioritization and Product Owner acceptance decisions remain subject to team review.

## LLM-assisted implementation and evaluation
Source inspection identified report-quality weaknesses involving stale snapshots and incomplete data-class coverage. We changed the V2 code to withhold metrics when customer data is stale or volume records are incomplete, validate duplicate and invalid rows, and display unavailable percentages safely. We added automated tests covering customer scope, blocker categories, denominator consistency, synthetic exposure calculations, missing entries, duplicate records, and invalid numeric values. GitHub Actions passed 18 tests and the production build, including UI-source contract checks of wording and structure. No production network monitoring or cryptographic enforcement is claimed.
